Privacy Policy

Last updated: 2 November 2026

Effective date: 2 November 2026

The short version. The Safari extension restyles pages on your device and never sends page content, the sites you visit or your browsing history anywhere. It contains no analytics, ads or tracking. The Darc app is free from version 1.1 and uses services from Google and RevenueCat: on iPhone and iPad, free users see ads from Google AdMob; Google Firebase measures how the app is used, reports crashes and delivers some settings; RevenueCat checks whether you have unlocked Darc. Where the law requires consent, ads and analytics wait for your answer in Google's consent form, and the advertising identifier is used only if you allow tracking when iOS asks. The Mac app shows no ads. If you bought Darc while it was a paid app, or you unlock it, the app shows you no ads. We have no account system and run no servers of our own.

This policy explains how the Darc app and its Safari extension for iPhone, iPad and Mac ("Darc", "the app") handle information, and the rights you have. It describes Darc 1.1 and later. Darc 1.0, the paid version, contained none of the Google or RevenueCat services described below. The policy also covers this website, darcapp.com.

Who we are

Darc is developed and published by Darius Cirjan, who is the data controller for the purposes of the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). You can reach us at iosallapps@icloud.com.

Privacy at a glance

  • Your browsing stays in Safari. No service in Darc receives page content, addresses or host names of the sites you visit, form data, passwords or your browsing history.
  • Ads: the free iPhone and iPad app shows full-screen ads from Google AdMob. The Mac app and the Safari extension show no ads. Nobody who unlocked Darc or bought the paid app is shown an ad, and no ad requests are made for them.
  • Analytics and crash reports: Google Firebase measures how the app is used, reports crashes and delivers some of the app's settings.
  • Purchases: Apple processes payments, and RevenueCat tells the app whether Darc is unlocked.
  • Your choices: where the law requires consent, for example in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, ads and analytics wait for your answer in Google's consent form. The advertising identifier is used only if you allow tracking when iOS asks.
  • We never ask for your name or email address, and we do not sell your personal information.

How the Safari extension works

To change how a website looks, the extension needs Safari's permission to access websites. When you allow it, Safari shows a warning that the extension can read and alter web pages. Darc uses that access only to do its job:

  • It reads a page's colours and styles inside Safari, on your device, and changes them to apply a dark or light theme.
  • To read a page's styles, it may load that page's own stylesheets or images again from the website that serves them. Those requests go to the website you are visiting, never to us.
  • It does not send page content, form data, passwords or your browsing history anywhere. The extension contains no analytics, advertising or crash reporting code, and there is no Darc server to send anything to.
  • It keeps your settings and site rules (for example, which mode a site uses) on your device so it can apply them.
  • To know which features to apply, it reads whether Darc is unlocked from the app's storage on your device and, from time to time, asks Apple's StoreKit on your device. It does not contact Google or RevenueCat.

Data stored on your device

Darc stores its settings on your device: the global mode, site rules with the host names of the sites you set a rule for, fine-tuning values, dark hours, and whether onboarding was completed. The extension also keeps a small amount of status on your device, such as when it last themed a page and the host name of the page it last handled, so the app can tell you whether the extension is running.

Recently themed sites. To open each site in the right theme without a white flash, the extension remembers, for each website it themed, the host name of the site (for example, example.com, not the full address or anything from the page), the last theme it used there, and whether the site failed to theme and when. This list is kept in the extension's own storage on your device, in every Safari window, private windows included. A site's entry is deleted automatically once the extension has not themed that site for 30 days. It is never sent to us or anywhere else, and it is not synced through iCloud.

Purchases and choices. The app also keeps on your device whether Darc is unlocked or was bought as a paid app, so the app and the extension work offline, the answers you gave in Google's consent form, and markers that stop one-time analytics events from being sent twice. None of this is synced through iCloud.

Removing it. To clear one site, tap Darc's button in Safari's toolbar on that site and choose Reset site; this also removes the site's rule. Deleting the Darc app removes the extension and everything it stored on that device. It does not cancel a subscription.

iCloud sync

If iCloud is turned on for your Apple Account, Darc syncs settings and site rules (the sites you chose a mode for) between your devices using Apple's iCloud key-value storage. This data is stored in your own iCloud account under Apple's terms and privacy policy. We cannot access it, and none of the services below receive it. You can turn this off in your device's iCloud settings. The list of recently themed sites described above is not part of this sync.

Data collected by the services in the app

The services below are part of the Darc app, not the Safari extension, and receive data directly from the app. None of them receive page content, the addresses or host names of the sites you visit, your site rules or your browsing history, and none of them receive your name, email address or contacts.

Advertising: Google AdMob (iPhone and iPad only)

The free iPhone and iPad app shows full-screen ads served by Google AdMob, when you open the app and at some points after you use it. Google receives:

  • Your IP address, from which Google can estimate your approximate location, such as your country or city.
  • Device and app information, such as device model, iOS version, language and app version.
  • Ad requests and interactions, such as which ads were shown and whether you tapped them.
  • Your device's advertising identifier (IDFA), only if you allow tracking when iOS asks.

Google uses this to serve ads, measure how they perform, prevent fraud and, where you have allowed it, show ads based on your interests.

When. Darc requests no ads until Google's consent form has been answered, where one is required, and until you have answered the iOS tracking prompt. If you unlock Darc, or you bought it while it was a paid app, the ad service is not started, no ad is requested and no consent form is shown. The Mac app does not include Google AdMob.

Personalisation. Where consent is required and you have not agreed to personalised ads, Darc asks Google for non-personalised ads. Anywhere in the world, if you do not allow tracking, the advertising identifier is not available and ads are not based on tracking you across other companies' apps and websites. Google may still use contextual information, such as the app the ad appears in and your approximate location. Darc also supports Apple's SKAdNetwork, which lets an advertiser learn that an install came from an ad without identifying you.

Google's privacy policy applies to the data it receives: policies.google.com/privacy. How Google uses data from apps that use its services is explained at policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites.

Usage analytics: Google Analytics for Firebase

Google Analytics for Firebase receives:

  • Events describing how the app is used, for example app opens and returns, onboarding steps, the first time Safari reports Darc working and the first site rule saved (never which site), the unlock and offer screens, purchases and restores with the plan, price and currency, whether an ad was shown, when the app asks for a rating, your answers to permission prompts and taps on some Help rows.
  • Whether you use the free version or have unlocked Darc, and the state of a subscription (for example in a trial, active or expired).
  • A random identifier for this installation of the app, the identifier Apple gives to apps from the same developer (IDFV), device and app information, and your IP address, from which Google estimates your approximate location.

We use this to understand which features work, fix what does not, and see whether the unlock and our marketing are working.

When. Analytics stays switched off until Darc knows whether consent is needed for you. On iPhone and iPad, where consent is required, it is switched on only if you agree in Google's consent form to information being stored on your device and to measurement. Elsewhere, it is switched on once Google has confirmed that no consent is needed, or once your App Store country is known to be outside the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland. On the Mac there is no consent form: analytics runs only when your App Store country is outside those regions, and stays off when it is inside them or cannot be determined. Events from before that decision are held in memory and are discarded if analytics stays off.

Advertising signals. Only if you allow tracking on iPhone or iPad may Google Analytics use your advertising identifier and share measurement data with Google's advertising services. If you do not allow tracking, and always on the Mac, Darc tells Google Analytics that these advertising uses are not permitted.

Crash reports: Firebase Crashlytics

When the app crashes, Firebase Crashlytics receives a report: the technical details of the crash, device model, operating system version, app version, device state such as free memory and storage, and a random installation identifier. If analytics is switched on, the report also includes the most recent analytics events. Crash reporting is not controlled by the consent form. It runs so that we can find and fix crashes, and it contains nothing from the pages you visit. The Safari extension does not include Crashlytics.

App settings: Firebase Remote Config

The app downloads a few of its settings from Firebase Remote Config: the layout of the unlock screen shown during onboarding, the plan it selects first, and the date Darc became free, which the app uses to recognise people who bought the paid app. The request includes a random installation identifier, the app version, your device language, country and time zone, and your IP address.

Firebase is provided by Google. More about how Firebase handles data is at firebase.google.com/support/privacy.

Purchases: Apple and RevenueCat

Darc can be unlocked with an auto-renewing subscription or a one-time purchase, sold through the App Store. Apple processes your payment, and your payment details never reach us or RevenueCat. RevenueCat receives:

  • A random, anonymous user identifier that RevenueCat creates for this installation.
  • Your App Store purchase information, such as which product you bought, purchase and expiry dates, whether you are in a free trial, the price and currency, and whether a subscription will renew.
  • Device and app information, such as operating system version, app version, language and App Store country, and your IP address.
  • Only while analytics is switched on, the random Firebase identifier of this installation, so that renewals, trial conversions and refunds can be counted in Google Analytics.

We use this to know whether Darc is unlocked, to restore your purchases, to check whether you can start a free trial or an offer, and to see how purchases are doing. RevenueCat does not receive your advertising identifier. RevenueCat's privacy policy is at revenuecat.com/privacy, and Apple's is at apple.com/legal/privacy.

If you bought the paid app. To recognise people who bought Darc while it was a paid app, the app reads the signed record of your original download that Apple's StoreKit keeps on your device: the version you first downloaded and when. This check happens on your device. Its answer is stored on your device and is not sent to us.

Report issue and email

"Report issue" in the extension's menu does not send anything by itself. It opens a pre-filled email in your mail app, addressed to us, containing the site's host name (not the full address or anything from the page), the mode and engine Darc used, whether Darc found the site already dark, the Darc version and the platform. You can read and edit the email, and it is sent only if you choose to send it.

If you write to us, we receive your email address and whatever you include in the message. We use it only to answer you and keep the conversation for as long as needed to help you, after which we delete it.

Legal basis (GDPR)

If you are in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, we rely on these legal bases:

  • Consent (Article 6(1)(a) GDPR) for advertising, including personalised ads, for Google Analytics, and for tracking through your advertising identifier. You give it in Google's consent form and in the iOS tracking prompt, and you can withdraw it at any time, as described under Your choices. Withdrawing consent does not affect processing that took place before.
  • Contract (Article 6(1)(b) GDPR) for processing your purchase and subscription status through Apple and RevenueCat, which is needed to provide the unlock you bought, and for recognising a purchase of the paid app.
  • Legitimate interests (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR) for crash reports and app settings, which we need to keep Darc working reliably, and for answering your emails. You can object to this processing by contacting us.

Tracking and App Tracking Transparency

  • During onboarding on iPhone and iPad, after Google's consent form if one is shown, iOS asks whether Darc may track you across other companies' apps and websites.
  • If you allow tracking, your advertising identifier is available to Google AdMob and Google Analytics for personalised ads and ad measurement, still subject to your choices in Google's consent form where one applies.
  • If you ask Darc not to track, the advertising identifier is not used, and Darc tells Google Analytics that advertising uses of your data are not permitted.
  • No ad is requested until you have answered the prompt.
  • You can change your answer at any time in the Settings app, under Privacy & Security, Tracking. Darc applies the change the next time you open it.
  • The Mac app does not ask, and does not use an advertising identifier.

Third parties

Darc shares data only with the services described above: Google (AdMob, Analytics, Crashlytics and Remote Config, under the Google Privacy Policy and Firebase's privacy information) and RevenueCat (RevenueCat Privacy Policy). Apple provides the App Store, Safari, StoreKit and iCloud; when you use them, Apple's privacy policy applies. Darc includes the open source Dark Reader engine, which runs inside the extension on your device and does not send data anywhere.

International transfers

Google and RevenueCat process data in the United States and in other countries. When data from the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland is transferred to a country without an adequacy decision, these companies protect it with safeguards recognised under the GDPR, such as the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework or the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses.

How long data is kept

  • Data on your device stays until you delete it, or, for recently themed sites, until the 30 days described above have passed. Deleting the app removes it from that device.
  • Google Analytics keeps event data for the retention period set in our Google Analytics account, which Google limits to at most 14 months. Reports that only show totals and do not identify anyone may be kept longer.
  • Firebase Crashlytics keeps crash reports and their identifiers for 90 days.
  • Firebase Remote Config and Google AdMob keep data for the periods set out in Firebase's privacy information and Google's privacy policy, linked above.
  • RevenueCat keeps purchase records for as long as they are needed to provide the unlock and to meet accounting and legal obligations.
  • Emails are kept for as long as needed to answer you and follow up, and then deleted.

When you visit it, GitHub receives your IP address and standard technical request data to deliver the pages and keep the service secure, and may keep them in its logs under the GitHub General Privacy Statement. Children

Darc is not directed to children under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal data from children under 13 or under the age of digital consent in their country. If you believe a child has used Darc and data about them has been collected, or a child has emailed us, a parent or guardian can write to us and we will help have it deleted.

Your choices

  • Consent form: if your region requires it, you can reopen Google's consent form at any time in the Darc app on iPhone or iPad, in the Help tab, under Ad Privacy Settings.
  • Tracking: the Settings app, under Privacy & Security, Tracking.
  • Ad personalisation with Google: myadcenter.google.com.
  • No ads at all: unlock Darc. People who bought the paid app already see none.
  • iCloud sync: your device's iCloud settings.
  • Everything on your device: delete the app. A subscription is not cancelled by deleting the app.

Your rights

Under the GDPR and similar laws you have the right to access, correct, delete or export personal data about you, to object to or restrict its processing, to withdraw consent at any time, and to complain to a data protection authority. In Romania this is the Autoritatea Națională de Supraveghere a Prelucrării Datelor cu Caracter Personal (ANSPDCP). We would appreciate the chance to help first.

Because Darc has no accounts, the data held by Google and RevenueCat is linked to random identifiers rather than to your name or email address, so we often cannot tell which records are yours. Email us at iosallapps@icloud.com and we will do what we can. For purchase records, include the order ID from your Apple receipt (you can find it at reportaproblem.apple.com) so that we can find your record and ask RevenueCat to delete it. Apple keeps its own purchase records under Apple's privacy policy. We answer privacy requests within one month.

California residents. We do not sell personal information for money. If you allow tracking, the advertising identifier and ad data that Google receives may count as "sharing" personal information for cross-context behavioural advertising under California law. You can stop this at any time by not allowing tracking in the Settings app, under Privacy & Security, Tracking. We do not use sensitive personal information, and you have the right to know about and delete your personal information and not to be treated differently for using these rights.

Security

The services above receive data over encrypted connections. No method of storing or sending data is completely secure, but we choose established providers and share only what this policy describes.

Changes to this policy

If Darc's handling of information changes, we will update this page and its effective date before the change takes effect. If a change is significant, we will also mention it in the app's release notes. If a change requires your consent, we will ask for it.
