Safari extension for iPhone, iPad and Mac

Dark when it’s late. Light when it’s not.

Darc gives bright websites a real dark theme, and gives dark-only websites a light one. It runs inside Safari and nowhere else.

Free to download. One optional purchase, no subscription.

Two directions

1:07 a.m.

You open a long article in bed. It’s white edge to edge. With Darc it arrives dark: backgrounds, text, borders and buttons recoloured from the page’s own styles, so it looks like the site designed it. The photo in the article keeps its colours.

12:30 p.m.

A forum you read every day only comes in black, and you are on a bright train. Darc Forever can turn that site light, just that one, and leave everything else alone.

Darc reads each page as it opens and only changes the ones that need it. A site that already has its own dark theme is left to use it.

How it works

  1. 1

    Install Darc and open it once.

  2. 2

    Turn it on in Safari.

    iPhone and iPad: Settings > Apps > Safari > Extensions > Darc. Turn on Allow Extension, then set Other Websites to Allow.

    Mac: Safari > Settings > Extensions, tick Darc, then Edit Websites and set Other Websites to Allow.

  3. 3

    Pick a mode.

    System follows your device’s appearance. Dark makes every bright site dark. Off leaves the web alone. Change a single site from Darc’s button in Safari’s toolbar.

The Darc app shows “Darc is on” once it has themed its first page, so you know it worked.

What it leaves alone

Darc changes colours, not content. It steps around the things you should see exactly as the site made them:

  • Photos, video and map tiles, which keep their own colours by default.
  • Card fields, Apple Pay, PayPal and Stripe forms.
  • CAPTCHA checks.

And if it gets a page wrong, for example text that ends up nearly the same colour as its background, Darc notices and removes everything it added. It tries another way of theming that page, and if none works, it shows the page as the site built it. A week later it gives the site a fresh try.

Questions

Safari says Darc “can read and alter webpages, including passwords”. Should I worry?

Safari shows that warning for every extension that changes how pages look, because to recolour a page an extension has to read it. Darc reads colours and styles on your device and sends nothing anywhere. It has no server to send to. If you prefer, allow it only on the sites you choose.

A site looks broken. What now?

Tap Darc’s button in Safari’s toolbar. Reset site clears that site’s rule. Disable for this site leaves it untouched from now on. Report issue opens an email with the site’s name, the mode and your Darc version. Nothing is sent unless you press send. More on the support page.

Will my photos look like negatives?

They shouldn’t. Darc recolours backgrounds and text from the page’s own styles and, by default, leaves images, video and maps in their original colours. On the rare page it can’t read that way, it falls back to a colour filter and shields photos from it. If one still looks wrong, Report issue in Darc’s menu tells me which site. With Darc Forever you can also dim photos a little on a site you read at night.

Will it slow pages down?

Darc gives itself 8 milliseconds at a time on a page, then steps back. If a busy page keeps it waiting, it backs off for longer, and in the end stops following that page’s changes rather than taking frames from it.

Why does a page sometimes flash white?

Safari paints its own white placeholder before the page exists, and no extension can reach it. After that, Darc darkens the page as early as Safari lets it run. On a site you have opened before, it remembers the choice and starts dark straight away.

Do I have to pay again on my Mac or iPad?

No. One purchase, all three, same Apple Account. On a new device use Restore Purchases in Darc’s Help tab.

Is it a subscription?

No. Darc is free to use, with one optional purchase that you pay once. The App Store shows its price in your currency.

Is Darc in German?

Yes. Darc launches in 45 languages, German included, and follows your device’s language.

Which devices?

iPhone and iPad with iOS 18 or later, and Macs with macOS 15 or later.
